Top 10 Universities That Give the Most Grants to American Students (2026-27)

By Wells Love · Published October 4, 2026 · 5 min read · Last reviewed October 4, 2026 · How we write

Grants are financial aid you never repay, and a growing number of elite universities now publish exact family-income lines below which tuition is free. Forbes reported in September 2026 that 21 private schools ranked in its top 50 now offer free-tuition guarantees, with families earning up to $250,000 qualifying at the most generous. Crucially, the benefit comes as institutional grants, not loans.

Total grant dollars depend on class size, so this editorial list ranks schools by how generous their published guarantees are to U.S. families: how high the income line for free tuition sits, and how low the line is for covering almost everything. All thresholds assume "typical assets," which usually means retirement accounts are ignored but large savings, brokerage accounts or business holdings may reduce aid. Most programs begin with a specific entering class, so check the dates.

The list

1

Princeton University

Free tuition for most families earning up to $250,000, and tuition, housing, food, books and personal expenses covered for families earning up to $150,000 (with typical assets). Princeton has offered loan-free aid since 2001 and uses its own aid form alongside the FAFSA.

2

University of Chicago

Beginning fall 2027, free tuition for families earning under $250,000 and free housing, meals and fees for those earning under $125,000, doubling its earlier guarantee.

3

Harvard University

Tuition-free since 2025 for families earning $200,000 or less. At $100,000 or less, Harvard covers tuition, fees, housing, food and extras such as health insurance and travel.

4

Massachusetts Institute of Technology

Since fall 2025, full tuition for families earning below $200,000, and the entire cost of attendance for families below $100,000. MIT says roughly 80% of American households fall under its tuition-free line.

5

Yale University

Beginning with the 2026-27 entering class, at least full tuition for families earning below $200,000. Families below $100,000 are expected to pay nothing toward tuition, housing, meals, travel or health insurance.

6

University of Pennsylvania

The Quaker Commitment guarantees at least a full-tuition package of grants and work-study, with no loans, for U.S. students from families earning up to $200,000. Families below $75,000 receive aid for tuition, fees, housing and dining. Penn no longer counts home equity.

7

Johns Hopkins University

From 2026-27, full tuition for domestic undergraduates from families earning $200,000 or less, and added coverage of fees and living expenses at $100,000 or less.

8

Vanderbilt University

Opportunity Vanderbilt guarantees full-tuition, loan-free aid to U.S. families earning $150,000 or less; the school reported median awards above the full cost of attendance for families under $50,000.

9

Northwestern University

Tuition-free for families earning $150,000 or less, and no-cost attendance at $70,000 or less. Northwestern has met full demonstrated need without loans since 2016.

10

University of Notre Dame

From 2026-27, zero tuition for families under $150,000 and half tuition below $200,000, with tuition, fees, housing and food covered below $60,000.

Also worth checking

The same Forbes survey lists many more schools with their own lines: Boston University (the BU Promise covers tuition, housing and food at $75,000 or less for the 2026-27 class), Colgate ($175,000), Dartmouth ($175,000), Emory ($200,000), Columbia ($150,000), Brown ($125,000), Caltech (full costs below $100,000), Rice (from fall 2027, $200,000), Swarthmore and Washington and Lee (both from 2027-28, $200,000) and NYU (tuition-free below $100,000 for first-year students at its New York campus). Duke has a separate guarantee for residents of North and South Carolina earning $150,000 or less.

The fine print most families miss

  • Admission comes first. Admission rates at these ten schools run roughly 4% to 11%, according to government data cited by Forbes. A grant guarantee does not make admission any easier.
  • Most students do not get free tuition. Forbes notes that at Princeton 31% of an incoming class received no aid at all, because student bodies skew wealthy.
  • Citizenship rules differ. Many guarantees apply to U.S. citizens and permanent residents; several schools also treat undocumented and DACA students as domestic for aid. International aid rules are separate.
  • Assets can change the result. Families with large non-retirement assets or businesses may receive less than the headline suggests.
  • Public schools mostly help residents. State flagships usually reserve their need-based programs for in-state students.

Federal and state grants for everyone

You do not need an elite school to receive grants. The federal Pell Grant maximum is $7,395 for 2026-27 (the minimum is $740), and it goes to students with the greatest financial need. State grants and state-funded programs, such as the need-based Carolina Covenant at UNC Chapel Hill or broad free-tuition programs in a few states, can matter as much as institutional aid. Our guide to cheap, high-quality public universities covers the public side.

How to apply for these grants

  1. Complete the FAFSA at studentaid.gov; the 2027-28 form opened in late September 2026.
  2. Check whether each school also requires the CSS Profile from the College Board (Princeton uses its own form instead).
  3. Gather tax returns, W-2s and records of savings and investments for each parent, including divorced or separated parents where the school requires both.
  4. Use each school's net price calculator to estimate aid before you apply.
  5. Compare award letters on net cost, then enter each into the College Cost & ROI Calculator using the aid field.
  6. Use the Pomodoro Timer to schedule focused 25-minute blocks for essays and forms ahead of early deadlines.

Final thoughts

These guarantees can turn a $95,000-a-year sticker price into a price many families can afford, but they are designed for a very small number of admitted students. Build a balanced list that includes a strong public university and one or two affordable options, and read our FAFSA and scholarship playbook to make sure every application is complete.
