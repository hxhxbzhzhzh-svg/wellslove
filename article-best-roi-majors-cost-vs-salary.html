College ROI & Finance

Which Majors Have the Best College ROI? A Cost-vs-Salary Comparison

By wellslovehub.com · Published October 4, 2026 · 5 min read · Last reviewed October 4, 2026 · How we write

Return on investment in college depends on two numbers: what you pay and what you earn. A major with a high starting salary can still be a poor deal at a very expensive school, while a lower-paying field can be sensible when the price is low. This guide compares eleven fields using the same method as our College Cost & ROI Calculator.

The method

Total cost is the full program cost. Net income is 75% of the starting salary (a simplified tax assumption). Payback is total cost divided by net income. We use the calculator's approximate entry-level salaries, not official statistics, so for real decisions check your field and city in the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook and College Scorecard.

We compare two round-figure scenarios close to the calculator's presets: $120,000 total (about a public in-state school with housing) and $248,000 total (about a private nonprofit school), with no aid.

The calculator labels payback of 4 years or less as High ROI, 4 to 8 years as Moderate and above 8 as Low.

What the table shows

  • School price moves the result more than major does at the low end. Education at a $120,000 total pays back in 3.6 years. At $248,000 it takes 7.3 years, twice as long.
  • High-paying fields stay in the High band even at private prices, although only computer science, law and finance stay under four years at $248,000 in these figures.
  • The same price is not the same risk. A $248,000 degree in a field with $45,000 salaries leaves little room for error.
  • Law is special. A JD comes after an undergraduate degree, so total cost is higher than the table suggests.

How to improve your own ROI

  1. Lower the cost: in-state public schools, grants, scholarships and the 2+2 path.
  2. Raise realistic income: internships, co-ops and a strong résumé (see our guide to paying for college with work).
  3. Finish on time: an extra year adds both cost and a delay in earnings.
  4. Match debt to salary: use the Loan Calculator and keep payments to a share of income you can live with.
  5. Run low, middle and high salary scenarios in the College Cost & ROI Calculator.

Public service and lower-paying careers

Fields such as teaching or social work often pay less at the start but may offer paths to loan forgiveness. The new Repayment Assistance Plan is a qualifying plan for Public Service Loan Forgiveness, but eligibility depends on your employer and payments, so confirm the rules at studentaid.gov before relying on it. Our student loan guide explains the 2026 changes.

Beyond the numbers

ROI is one input. Strength in a subject, interest in the daily work, job satisfaction, location and long-term growth matter too. If you are drawn to finance, our guide to the top finance specializations shows how varied that field is.

Final thoughts

Treat the numbers as a conversation starter, not a verdict. A good decision pairs an affordable program with a major you can excel in and a realistic plan for experience and jobs.
