Career Paths & Internships

Best US Companies for Finance Internships

By Wells Love · Published October 2, 2026 · 3 min read · Last reviewed October 2, 2026 · How we write

A finance internship is often the first serious step toward a full-time offer. At many large employers, the summer program is the main pipeline for graduate hiring, so your performance and relationships during those weeks can matter more than your grades. The firms below run well-known, structured programs across banking, markets, asset management and quantitative roles.

What makes a strong internship program

Look for a defined project, an assigned mentor, regular feedback and a clear path to a return offer. Large programs usually include training in financial modeling, accounting basics and industry knowledge, then place interns on real teams. Ask during interviews how many interns received full-time offers last year, because conversion rates show how seriously a firm invests in its interns.

Recruiting timelines start early

Applications for many finance programs open months before the summer, and some employers recruit sophomores for early insight or diversity programs. Missing an early deadline can limit your options, so build a calendar for each firm. Prepare a clean resume and test it with our ATS Resume Analyzer before submitting.

Preparing for interviews

Expect questions on your resume, basic accounting, valuation concepts and market awareness, along with behavioral questions about teamwork and pressure. Practice explaining your experience with specific results, and follow current markets so you can discuss a recent deal or trend with confidence.

Beyond the famous names

Boutique banks, regional firms and corporate finance teams also run excellent programs and sometimes offer more hands-on work. A smaller internship with real responsibility can teach more than a large one where tasks are narrow. Compare programs on learning and mentorship, not logo alone.

Plan the financial side

Internships can pay well, but relocation costs are real. Estimate your longer-term return with the College ROI Calculator and use the CV Builder to turn each experience into clear, quantified bullet points.

1

Goldman Sachs

Goldman Sachs runs large summer analyst programs in investment banking, markets and other divisions, with structured training and rotations in some areas. Recruiting starts early, often a year before the internship. Strong preparation for technical interviews and clear reasons for choosing the firm matter.

2

Morgan Stanley

Morgan Stanley is well regarded for its analyst programs in banking, sales and trading and wealth management. Interns usually work on live team projects and are evaluated for full-time offers. Applicants should learn the differences between divisions and explain which they prefer and why.

3

JPMorgan Chase

JPMorgan Chase operates one of the largest internship programs across investment banking, markets, asset management and technology. Because of its size, the variety of roles is wide, and applicants can choose among many locations. Read each posting closely, as skills and interview formats differ by division.

4

BlackRock

BlackRock offers internships in investment management, risk analytics and technology, reflecting its role as a very large asset manager and the maker of the Aladdin risk platform. It suits students interested in portfolios and data. Programming and analytical skills are valuable alongside finance coursework.

5

Citigroup

Citigroup runs summer programs in banking and global markets and has a notably international footprint. Interns are usually placed in a specific team and exposed to the business through projects and training sessions. It is worth researching the particular group you apply to rather than writing a generic application.

6

Bank of America

Bank of America offers internships across investment banking, global markets, wealth management and technology, with a broad US presence. Its programs suit students who want a large-bank environment and many team choices. Show interest in a specific business line and prepare to discuss markets with confidence.

7

Jane Street

Jane Street is a quantitative trading firm known for technical, puzzle-based interviews and an emphasis on probability and problem-solving. Internships suit students with strong mathematics, computer science or related skills. It is a different style of preparation from a traditional banking application.

8

Citadel

Citadel and its related trading business run competitive programs in quantitative research, trading and engineering. Applicants are typically strong in mathematics, statistics and programming. Expect a rigorous selection process, and prepare through practice problems and coding exercises rather than finance coursework alone.

9

Evercore

Evercore is an advisory-focused boutique investment bank, so interns often work in small teams on mergers, restructuring and strategic advice. Boutiques can give junior people exposure to deals earlier, but programs are smaller and hiring is selective. Networking and clear interest in advisory work help.

10

Blackstone

Blackstone is a leading private-markets investor with analyst roles in private equity, real estate and credit. These positions are highly selective and many candidates come from prior banking or other finance experience. Check the firm's own careers page for timing, which differs from traditional banking cycles.

What makes an internship valuable

The best programs give real responsibility, structured training, access to senior people and a clear path to a full-time offer. Brand names help, but a smaller firm that lets you build models or manage client work can teach more than a large program with limited tasks.

Types of employers

Large banks offer formal training classes and recruiting pipelines. Asset managers and hedge funds are smaller, more selective and research-driven. Insurers, corporates and fintech companies provide strong finance and data experience, often with better balance. Consider which environment suits you.

How to apply

Track opening dates, prepare a one-page CV and tailor each application. Use the CV Builder for a clean layout and the ATS Resume Analyzer to check keywords against the posting. Practice behavioral and technical interviews, and follow up politely after each stage.

Before you accept

Compare the full picture: location, pay, housing costs, start dates and conversion rates, which are often shared by career services. If you receive more than one offer, ask for reasonable time to decide and respond promptly and courteously to all employers.

Make the most of it

Arrive prepared, take notes, ask for feedback weekly and deliver accurate work on time. Build relationships across teams, and write down your accomplishments with numbers, which will strengthen your next application.
