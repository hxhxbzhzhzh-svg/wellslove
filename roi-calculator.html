How this calculator works

Gross yearly cost is the sum of ten recurring categories (everything except the one-time Application / Enrollment Fees). Net yearly cost subtracts your yearly aid. Total program cost = net yearly cost × years of study + one-time fees. Net income is 75% of gross salary (a flat, simplified tax assumption) and payback = total program cost ÷ net income. High ROI is 4 years or less, Moderate 4 to 8, Low above 8. Results are estimates; they ignore loan interest and real taxes. See BLS and College Scorecard for data.

The 11 cost categories

  • Tuition & Fees – the published price; check whether mandatory fees are included.
  • Room & Board / Housing – dorm or rent.
  • Food / Meal Plan – campus plan plus groceries.
  • Books & Supplies – textbooks and course materials.
  • Transportation – commuting, parking, trips home.
  • Personal Expenses – phone, clothing, laundry, entertainment.
  • Health Insurance – school plan, or 0 if covered elsewhere.
  • Technology / Laptop / Software – spread the cost across your years.
  • Student Fees – lab, course, activity, technology and parking fees.
  • Application / Enrollment Fees – one-time total, not multiplied by years.
  • Other Expenses – anything that does not fit above.

Not sure what each line should be? Read The True Cost of College: 11 Expenses to Budget and Hidden College Costs.

A worked example

Take the default public in-state budget: about $30,500 gross per year, no aid, four years, so about $122,000 in total plus $100 in one-time fees. With a $60,000 salary the net income is $45,000 and payback is roughly 2.7 years. Now enter $12,000 of yearly aid: net cost drops to about $18,500 a year and payback falls to about 1.6 years. Switch to the private preset with no aid and payback rises to roughly 5.8 years. Aid and school choice move the result more than almost any other input.

FAQ

Is the result guaranteed?

No. It is an estimate from the figures you enter.

Does it model loan interest?

No. Use the Loan Calculator for financing costs; the button above sends it your total.

Where do the preset numbers come from?

Tuition and housing use 2025-26 College Board averages; the other lines are round placeholders. Always replace them with your school's own cost-of-attendance page.
