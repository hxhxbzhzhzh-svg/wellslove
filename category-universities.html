About this section

Rankings can be a useful starting point, but they rarely answer the question students actually have: which school is the best choice for my subject, my budget and my goals? The guides here compare universities by cost, financial aid, research strength and career outcomes, and they explain what each list measures so you can judge how much weight to give it.

What we look at

  • Cost and aid: published tuition, typical net price and how generous a school is with grants.
  • Academic strength: strengths in fields such as finance, computer science and AI, not only overall reputation.
  • Outcomes: graduation rates, internships and the employers that recruit on campus.
  • Fit: location, class size and entry requirements.

Lists marked "editorial" are our own judgment and not a scored index. When we cite a ranking, we name its publisher. Before you apply, compare the real price of your shortlist with the College Cost & ROI Calculator and read about scholarships and financial aid.
